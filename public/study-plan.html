1. Study Design Overview

Phase 1 Pre-Reading Setup
5 min
Phase 2 Active Reading
45 min
Phase 3 Post-Reading + Survey
25 min

Each of the 12 participants completes two sessions (one CoRead, one Baseline) separated by at least one week. Order is counterbalanced: 6 participants do CoRead first, 6 do Baseline first. Two papers of matched complexity are used, also counterbalanced.

CoRead sessions use a Wizard-of-Oz confederate protocol: the researcher operates two additional authenticated accounts in separate browser windows, simulating peer collaborators following a standardized behavioral script. This ensures every participant experiences an identical collaborative context — validated practice in groupware research (Gutwin & Greenberg, 1998).

Why within-subjects with N=12? Within-subjects design controls individual differences and doubles statistical power at small N. Wilcoxon Signed-Rank tests are appropriate (non-parametric, paired, N=12). Standard at UIST/CHI for lab-based systems evaluation.

4. Category B — AI Timing Accuracy

This is CoRead's most novel and publishable metric. No prior paper has measured whether AI assistance arrived at the right moment in a reading session. Three complementary methods triangulate the answer.

Method 1
Log-based
Time from CIM trigger to participant's next action. Short gap = timely.
Method 2
Behavioral
Notification accepted vs dismissed. Acceptance = well-timed signal.
Method 3
Retrospective
Participant rates each notification after session: well-timed / too early / too late.

B1. Notification Outcome Breakdown

OutcomeDefinitionN events%
Clicked (engaged)Participant opened notification within 30s——

B2. Timing Accuracy by Struggle Severity

Compare acceptance rate when CIM fired at medium severity (score 0.72–0.88) vs. high severity (score >0.88). If high severity → higher acceptance rate, this validates the CIM threshold calibration.

B3. Retrospective Timing Ratings

After the session, each participant sees a timeline of every notification that fired during their session and rates each: Well-timed / Too early / Too late / I didn't notice it. Reported as percentage distribution across all ratings from all 12 participants.

Why this matters to reviewers Prior work on AI assistance timing (Horvitz 1999) identified timing as the central challenge of mixed-initiative systems but provided no empirical measurement method. CoRead's CIM gives a formal trigger; this data is the first empirical test of whether that trigger actually fires at the right moment.

The "opted back in" column is your most interesting finding A participant who turns a feature off and then re-enables it demonstrates experiential trust-building — they needed to see the system behave before trusting it with their data. This is a design insight no survey question can capture.

Privacy Sensitivity Hierarchy

Rank features by opt-out rate. This validates the formative finding that confusion signals carry higher social cost than content signals.

Why archetypes matter Showing that CoRead accommodated multiple reading styles — rather than forcing a single interaction model — is a strong design argument. It addresses the reviewer concern: "What about participants who don't want AI help?" The archetype data shows those participants can use CoRead successfully in annotation-first mode.

10. Category H — Qualitative Findings

Semi-structured interview (15 min) conducted once after both sessions. Analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke) with two coders. Target: 4–6 themes with representative quotes.

Interview Questions

  1. "Walk me through a moment in the CoRead session where the AI did something that felt helpful or unhelpful."
  2. "Did the role assignment change how you read compared to the other session?"
  3. "Was there a moment where you wished AI had stepped in but it didn't? Or stepped in when you didn't want it to?"
  4. "How did the post-reading summary affect your group discussion?"
  5. "You turned off [X feature] during the session — can you tell me why?" (only if they opted out of something)
  6. "Would you use CoRead in your actual reading group? What would you change?"

Expected Themes (grounded in formative study)

  • Epistemic accountability — role assignment creates responsibility to read deeply
  • Trust calibration — comfort with AI monitoring develops during the session, not before
  • Confusion visibility — seeing confusion signals reduces social cost of asking for help
  • Artifact-anchored discussion — post-reading synthesis changes how discussion starts
  • Attention economy — relevance-filtered notifications preserved focus better than expected

Important for small N With N=12, report all tests as exploratory rather than confirmatory. State: "Given the small sample size, we report Wilcoxon tests and effect sizes as exploratory indicators; findings should be interpreted with appropriate caution and validated in larger deployments." Reviewers expect this honesty and will penalize papers that over-claim statistical significance at N=12.
